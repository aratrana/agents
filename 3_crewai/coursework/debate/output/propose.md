Strict regulation of large language models is essential because their unchecked deployment poses significant risks that threaten public safety, democratic integrity, and societal well‑being. First, LLMs can generate highly persuasive falsehoods at scale—deepfake text, fabricated news, or targeted disinformation campaigns—that outpace human fact‑checking capabilities and erode trust in information ecosystems. Without legal safeguards, malicious actors exploit this capability to manipulate elections, incite violence, or undermine public health efforts. Second, these models inherit and amplify biases present in their training data, leading to discriminatory outcomes in hiring, lending, law‑enforcement advice, and medical recommendations when deployed without oversight. Third, the concentration of powerful LLMs in a few private entities creates asymmetrical power dynamics; absent regulation, corporations may prioritize profit over accountability, leaving users vulnerable to privacy violations, exploitation, or harmful content generation. Finally, the rapid pace of AI advancement outstrips existing ethical guidelines, making voluntary self‑regulation insufficient. Enacting clear, enforceable standards—such as mandatory impact assessments, transparency disclosures, usage‑restriction licenses, liability frameworks, and independent auditing—will ensure that LLMs serve the public good while mitigating their capacity for harm. Therefore, strict legal regulation is not merely prudent but necessary to protect individuals, preserve democratic processes, and steer technological progress responsibly.
